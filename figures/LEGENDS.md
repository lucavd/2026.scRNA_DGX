## Figure S1

Step-level wall time decomposition for CPU vs single-GPU at 10k, 50k, and 100k cells, mean of five repeats (first repeat included); the CPU and GPU panels have separate time axes. On CPU, DE testing and UMAP dominate; on GPU, the longest step, DE testing at 100k cells, takes 6.4 s on average. CPU neighbour search ran on one Numba thread, the Scanpy default (see Methods).

## Figure S2

Peak memory usage across dataset sizes, mean of five repeats (first repeat included); error bars: SD. Left: CPU RAM; right: aggregate GPU VRAM summed over the allocated devices (for 2, 4 and 8 GPUs dominated by the RMM pools pre-allocated by each Dask worker). CPU RAM grows roughly linearly; GPU VRAM is approximately constant for single-GPU runs due to RMM pool pre-allocation. All repeats of a configuration ran in one process; in the multi-GPU runs VRAM grew from the first to later repeats.

## Figure S3

Total pipeline wall time on three Visium platforms: CPU reference (one DGX H100 node, 100 CPUs) and GPU (one RTX 4090), mean of five repeats, each in a fresh process; error bars: SD. Labels give the speedup (ratio of means). At Visium HD 8 um the bars include co-occurrence, whose GPU result with rapids-singlecell 0.14.1 was incorrect (see text); the dashed lines mark the totals without it (6.9x). Visium HD 2 um was randomly subsampled to 400,000 bins (389,492 after QC) to fit in the 24 GiB of the RTX 4090.
